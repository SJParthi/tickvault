The answer

Why the two columns can never be equal

Dhan sends the exchange's Last Traded Time — the moment the trade was matched at NSE, truncated to a whole second. We stamp received_at ourselves from the system clock the instant the packet arrives. A gap is not an error; a gap of zero would be physically impossible.

This also explains the second thing you noticed

You said the close price only shows up "when received_at is finally closing that minute". That is exactly the 1-second flight time. The trade that closes 09:16 happens at 09:16:59 and lands on our machine at 09:17:00.2 — so if you read down the received_at column, minute 09:16's closing price appears at the top of minute 09:17. Dhan's chart and our candle both file it under 09:16, because both bucket on trade time, not arrival time. You were watching the system agree with Dhan, not disagree with it.

The trap

Do not read that gap as "our lag"

This is the single most misleading number in the database, and it is worth understanding before you look at another row. received_at − ts is two things added together: how long the packet took to reach us, plus how long ago that instrument last traded. Only the first is our problem.

The honest limit — and it is Dhan's, not ours

Adani's traded volume jumped 6,147 shares inside one second (09:15:42) and arrived as a single update carrying one price. Dhan's own architecture diagram says it: slow consumers "catch up with the latest available state". Their India feed is a conflated ~1-per-second snapshot with no sequence number — every individual print inside a second is merged before it ever reaches us, and no counter we can build will ever see them. "Not one tick missed" can only honestly mean "not one received packet lost". Anyone who promises you more than that is guessing.
